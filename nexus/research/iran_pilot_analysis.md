# Iran pilot: first thematic analysis

**Dataset:** 20 manually documented observations, collected 2026-11-23.  
**Status:** exploratory screening only; all source records remain unreviewed.  
**Method:** six explicit human-curated themes. Theme counts are record counts, not counts of independent customers, market size, or verified prevalence. Publisher-group counts are a conservative proxy, not proof that publishers independently investigated each underlying event.

## 1. Digital business continuity and customer access
- Evidence records: IR-0009 through IR-0015 (7 records).
- Publisher groups in dataset: 4 (Femena, RFE/RL, Iran International, The National).
- What sources describe: online sellers, home-based businesses, instructors and freelancers losing access to customers, sales channels, work or income during connectivity restrictions.
- Additional external corroboration located during this analysis: Associated Press coverage of small businesses losing access to customers and income during the 2026 shutdown; a UN Special Rapporteur report excerpt describes SME revenue losses and disruptions to e-commerce, payments and logistics. These sources are not yet added as dataset records.
- Important limitation: several Iran International records cover related shutdown periods and should not be treated as fully independent incidents. The evidence supports a continuity problem, not demand for any particular software.
- Candidate hypothesis: an offline-first microbusiness continuity kit: local product catalogue, order ledger, customer follow-up queue, printable/exportable order slips and automatic sync when connectivity returns. Do not promise online messaging or payment functions during a complete outage.
- Unknowns: actual usage of paper/Excel/WhatsApp backups; willingness to pay; acceptable data-storage risk; phone and connectivity constraints; whether merchants want a standalone tool.

## 2. Agricultural water, input-cost and production risks
- Evidence records: IR-0001, IR-0007, IR-0016, IR-0017, IR-0018 (5 records).
- Publisher groups: 3 (World Bank, BTI, FAO).
- What sources describe: water scarcity and drought risks, low reservoir levels, fertilizer and seed costs, crop production uncertainty, and demand for practical technical knowledge.
- Fresh FAO GIEWS brief (reference date 2026-11-10) says low reservoir levels could constrain irrigation in key producing provinces and high fertilizer/seed costs may discourage planting; it also reports that the 2026 cereal harvest recovered to near-average levels. This nuance matters: do not describe every crop or season as being in crisis.
- Candidate hypothesis: a localized farm decision-support/advisory service that helps a defined crop group track irrigation schedules, input costs and trusted agronomic guidance. It must be co-designed with local agronomists and avoid unsupported yield promises.
- Unknowns: crop- and province-specific willingness to pay, data availability, agronomic liability, distribution partners, and whether farmers already receive equivalent advice.

## 3. SME finance, technology and management constraints
- Evidence records: IR-0002, IR-0004, IR-0005 (3 records).
- Publisher groups: 3 (World Bank, Magiran study, Journal of Policy Modeling / ScienceDirect).
- What sources describe: financial constraints, inflation, technology and training gaps, and barriers to sales growth. A 2026 peer-reviewed study reports that sanctions harm sales growth and that access to finance, technology and management education are associated with growth.
- Candidate hypothesis: a practical SME operations and finance-readiness service: basic cash-flow visibility, inventory and margin tracking, and preparation of better-structured records for financing or business decisions. It cannot create credit access or guarantee growth.
- Unknowns: the most painful workflow, existing accounting tools, owner time, ability to pay, and whether a service or lightweight software is preferred.

## Screening result
1. **Digital business continuity** — strongest documented cross-publisher signal in this small dataset; best first customer-discovery target.
2. **Agricultural water/input decision support** — substantial public-interest problem and several source groups; needs a tightly defined crop/province and expert partner.
3. **SME operations/finance readiness** — credible research basis, but broad and not yet a sharply defined buyer problem.

These are ranked research hypotheses, not validated business opportunities. No revenue, market size, customer count, price, cost, or profitability estimate is asserted.

## First validation experiment
Interview 5–8 operators in one narrow segment (for example, Instagram-based home-product sellers) without collecting sensitive personal information. Ask:
1. What exact work stopped or became harder during the last connectivity disruption?
2. What workaround did they actually use?
3. How often does this happen, and what measurable loss or time cost did it create?
4. What tool or service do they already pay for, if any?
5. Would they test a simple offline order ledger? What would make it worth paying for?
6. Can they demonstrate the workflow using dummy data?

Record direct quotes only with consent. Separate observed facts, respondent claims, and NEXUS hypotheses. A stated interest is not payment evidence; seek a paid pilot or deposit only when lawful, safe and practical.

## Technical caveat
The previous exact-text analyst could not discover semantic recurrence because all 20 problem statements were worded differently. This update adds explicit human-curated theme labels. The analyst groups by those labels and clearly records `classification_method=explicit_curated_theme`. It is not autonomous semantic discovery. The theme assignment itself must be reviewed.
